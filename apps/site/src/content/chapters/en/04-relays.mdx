You have a <Term id="keypair" locale="en">keypair</Term> and you know how to sign an
<Term id="event" locale="en">event</Term>. But a signed note sitting on your laptop is a diary,
not a social network. To reach other people it has to travel somewhere. On Nostr, that somewhere
is a <Term id="relay" locale="en">relay</Term>.

## Relays are dumb (and that's a compliment)

Picture a relay as a **community notice board** in a café. Anyone can pin a card on it, anyone
can walk up and read the cards, and the café owner decides house rules: no ads, members only,
cards taken down after a month. That's it. The notice board doesn't know your friends, doesn't
rank your feed, and doesn't talk to the notice board in the café across the street.

Your <Term id="client" locale="en">client</Term> (the app you use) is the opposite: it's your
**personal assistant**. It knows which notice boards your friends use, walks to several of them,
collects the cards, throws away duplicates, checks every <Term id="signature" locale="en">signature</Term>,
and lays everything out nicely for you.

This split is on purpose:

- **Relays stay simple**, so they're cheap to run. Thousands of people run one, from big public
  relays to a tiny one in someone's closet.
- **Clients hold the intelligence**, so *you* choose the algorithm, not a company.
- **Nothing depends on one relay.** If a board disappears, your assistant just reads another one.
  That's where Nostr's <Term id="censorship-resistance" locale="en">censorship resistance</Term> comes from.

<Callout tone="tip" locale="en" title="Relays can't lie about what you said">
A relay can refuse your note, lose it, or hide it. What it can't do is *edit* it or post in your
name: every event carries your signature, and clients verify it on their own device (chapter 3).
The worst a relay can do is stay silent, and that's why we use more than one.
</Callout>

## Eavesdropping on the wire

Clients and relays talk over a <Term id="websocket" locale="en">WebSocket</Term>: a phone line
that stays open, so both sides can speak whenever they like. Every message on that line is a tiny
JSON array whose first item is a shouty uppercase word: the **verb**.

## The whole vocabulary fits on a sticky note

[NIP-01](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/01.md) defines the core language. Your
client says three things:

| Client says | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `REQ` | "Send me events matching these <Term id="filter" locale="en">filters</Term>, and keep me posted." Opens a <Term id="subscription" locale="en">subscription</Term> with a name you pick. |
| `EVENT` | "Please store this signed event." (Publishing.) |
| `CLOSE` | "I'm done with that subscription." |

The relay answers with five:

| Relay says | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `EVENT` | "Here's an event for your subscription called *feed*." |
| `EOSE` | "That's everything I had stored." <Term id="eose" locale="en">End Of Stored Events</Term>: stop the spinner. The subscription stays open for brand-new events. |
| `OK` | "About that event you sent: stored (`true`) or refused (`false`), and here's why." |
| `CLOSED` | "I'm ending your subscription," with a reason. |
| `NOTICE` | A human-readable message, like a note taped to the door. |

And there's one extra pair for logging in, from <Term id="nip42" locale="en">NIP-42</Term>: `AUTH`.
The relay sends a random challenge; your client signs it with your key and sends it back. No
password ever crosses the wire, only a signature. (There's also `COUNT` from
[NIP-45](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/45.md), for "how many?" questions, but
you rarely see it.)

<Drawer client:visible testid="ch04-uth-frames" locale="en" title="Under the hood: the exact frames">

Every frame is a JSON array sent as one WebSocket text message.

```json
["REQ", "feed", {"authors": ["e550c6e9…"], "kinds": [1], "limit": 2}]
["EVENT", "feed", {"id": "…", "pubkey": "…", "created_at": 1735686600, "kind": 1, "tags": [], "content": "…", "sig": "…"}]
["EOSE", "feed"]
["CLOSE", "feed"]

["EVENT", {"id": "…", "kind": 1, "…": "…"}]
["OK", "<event id>", true, ""]
["OK", "<event id>", false, "pow: difficulty 16 or more is required"]
["CLOSED", "feed", "rate-limited: slow down, too many requests"]
["NOTICE", "heads up: this relay restarts at 03:00 UTC"]
```

Rules worth knowing, straight from [NIP-01](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/01.md):

- The **subscription id** is any non-empty string up to 64 characters, chosen by the client and
  scoped to one connection. Sending a new `REQ` with an id that's already open **replaces** that
  subscription.
- A `REQ` may carry several filters; they're combined with OR. `limit` only applies to the first,
  stored batch. With `limit: 0` the relay skips stored events entirely and just streams new ones.
- After `EOSE`, the relay keeps sending matching events in real time until you `CLOSE`, it sends
  `CLOSED`, or the socket drops.
- A relay **must** answer every published `EVENT` with `OK`, and must send `CLOSED` when it refuses
  a `REQ`.
- `OK` and `CLOSED` messages start with a machine-readable prefix: `duplicate:`, `pow:`, `blocked:`,
  `rate-limited:`, `invalid:`, `restricted:`, `mute:` or `error:`. NIP-42 adds `auth-required:`.
  Note that `["OK", id, true, "duplicate: …"]` is a success: the relay already had it.

</Drawer>

<Drawer client:visible testid="ch04-uth-auth" locale="en" title="Under the hood: logging in with AUTH (NIP-42)">

The relay may send a challenge at any time, typically right after you connect or just before it
refuses something:

```json
["AUTH", "ostrich-7f3a9c2e"]
["CLOSED", "dms", "auth-required: gift wraps are only served to their recipient"]
```

The client answers with a signed, **never published** event of kind `22242`, tagging the relay URL
and the challenge. Relays check the signature, the challenge, the URL, and that `created_at` is
recent (roughly within 10 minutes):

```json
["AUTH", {"kind": 22242, "tags": [["relay", "wss://relay.delta.example"], ["challenge", "ostrich-7f3a9c2e"]], "content": "", "…": "id, pubkey, created_at, sig"}]
["OK", "<auth event id>", true, ""]
```

If you're logged in but your key still isn't allowed, the relay uses the `restricted:` prefix
instead. Paid relays and relays serving private <Term id="gift-wrap" locale="en">gift wraps</Term>
(chapter 8) rely on this. Spec: [NIP-42](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/42.md).

</Drawer>

<Drawer client:visible testid="ch04-uth-code" locale="en" title="Under the hood: talking to relays with nostr-tools">

`SimplePool` manages one WebSocket per relay and fans your requests out to all of them, deduplicating
events by id for you:

```ts
import { SimplePool } from "nostr-tools/pool";
import { finalizeEvent, generateSecretKey } from "nostr-tools/pure";

const relays = ["wss://relay.damus.io", "wss://nos.lol", "wss://relay.primal.net"];
const pool = new SimplePool();

// REQ to every relay; onevent fires once per unique event, oneose when all relays sent EOSE.
const sub = pool.subscribeMany(relays, { kinds: [1], limit: 3 }, {
  onevent: (event) => console.log(event.pubkey.slice(0, 8), event.content),
  oneose: () => sub.close(), // sends CLOSE to each relay
});

// Publishing = one EVENT per relay; each promise settles with that relay's OK.
const event = finalizeEvent(
  { kind: 1, created_at: Math.floor(Date.now() / 1000), tags: [], content: "hello relays" },
  generateSecretKey(),
);
const results = await Promise.allSettled(pool.publish(relays, event));
console.log(results.map((r) => r.status)); // e.g. ["fulfilled", "fulfilled", "rejected"]
```

For a single connection there's `Relay.connect(url)` from `nostr-tools/relay`, with `relay.subscribe`,
`relay.publish` and `relay.auth(sign)` for NIP-42. Under the hood it's exactly the frames you saw above.

Bonus: a relay can describe itself. Fetch its URL over HTTPS with the header
`Accept: application/nostr+json` and you get a JSON document with its name, contact, supported NIPs
and limits (<Term id="nip11" locale="en">NIP-11</Term>, [spec](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/11.md)).

</Drawer>

## Don't put all your notes in one relay

Because relays don't copy events between each other, your client usually sends every note to
**several** relays at once. If one goes offline, gets overloaded, or decides it doesn't like you,
your followers can still find your note on the others. Some relays are free, some are
<Term id="paid-relay" locale="en">paid</Term> (a small fee is a great <Term id="spam" locale="en">spam</Term>
filter), and each has its own house rules. Mix and match.

Try it: choose where to publish, then start knocking relays offline.

<RedundancyLab client:visible locale="en" />

How does Bob's client know *which* relays to check for your notes? You publish a small list of
"where I write" relays, and his client goes there. That's the
<Term id="outbox-model" locale="en">outbox model</Term>
([NIP-65](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/65.md)), and it's coming up in chapter 7.

<Callout tone="info" locale="en" title="Relays have rules, and that's fine">
`OK false` isn't an error in the protocol: it's a relay exercising its right to choose. One relay
might demand <Term id="proof-of-work" locale="en">proof of work</Term>, another only accepts members,
a third deletes everything after a week. Since no relay is special, you simply pick the ones whose
rules you like.
</Callout>
